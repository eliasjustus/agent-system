# Research: studies of approaches

For a study that asks which approaches exist and which to pursue, where the answer decides a design
or the next experiments. A routine choice inside a step (a library, a flag, a known fix) follows the
ordinary rungs instead.

## Before the choice

1. Read the project's ledger of approaches tried (one row each: source, tried or not, figures,
   pointer, date); start it if missing. Name the areas, and per area write what you already know.
2. Survey each area: at least three open searches worded as the problem, naming no paper, tool,
   product or author and not built around a known method; then named look-ups; at least one source
   read as full text. Queries hold nothing about the user or the project.
3. Reading levels, only these: *full text* (downloaded and read as text; the row says which parts),
   *page summary*, *search summary*. A proposal rests only on full text. Download and read in
   separate short commands, so the log shows both.
4. Per area at least three structurally different candidate lines, each marked *known before*,
   *found in the survey* (with its source) or *own idea*, and checked against the ledger: a
   parameter change of a tried line is not new. At least one line not known before, or "none found"
   with the queries.
5. Count per area from the log (open searches, full texts, candidates), write the counts, then
   `Direction chosen: <UTC>`. The order cannot be repaired afterwards.

Statements about the literature ("nothing exists", "only", "no newer") need three differently worded
searches, one in a more thorough mode where the tool has one; otherwise say what the search returned.
"Considered" needs a survey row or a measurement; otherwise "not looked at".

Notes, kept as you go: a survey table (time read from the clock or log, area, query or URL, open /
named / source, level and parts read, what it gave) and a candidate table (area, line, origin,
nearest ledger row, chosen and why).

## After the choice

Experiments as any check: the rule before any figure, held-out halves run by an independent check,
failed lines kept with their figures; where one number judges, log every attempt. Append every line
tried or rejected to the ledger. Name the nearest prior work; do not rate the result's novelty.

Before delivery, two checks by sessions without author context: the **process**, from the
transcript's search log, the notes, the new ledger rows and the report (survey before the choice,
open searches open, levels matching the log, rows for every "considered" and absence statement,
three candidates per area checked against the ledger, ledger appended); and the **text**, the report
against its sources (every unsupported, overstated or contradicted statement, absence claims first,
arithmetic recomputed). Name both verdicts in the delivery.
